How to scope an MVP in two weeks

ARArun Raj · Sep 24, 2026 · 6 min read
🎯
On this page
    Share
    LinkedInX

    Most MVPs grow too big before anyone has used them. The fix is not a better spreadsheet. It is a sharper question: what is the smallest thing that proves people want this?

    Start with one user and one job

    Pick a single type of user and the one job they hire your product to do. Every feature either helps that job or waits. It sounds obvious, but it removes most of the wish list in a single afternoon.

    Rank features by risk, not by effort

    List your assumptions and mark the ones that would sink the product if they were wrong. Build the features that test those first. A rough prototype that answers a risky question beats a polished feature nobody asked for.

    • Will people pay or commit their time?
    • Can we reach them affordably?
    • Can we build the hard part reliably?

    Cut it down to a two-week plan

    Turn the shortlist into a plan with a clear end date and a demo at the end of each week. If a feature does not fit, it moves to version two, written down so nobody forgets it.

    Key takeaway

    Write your MVP as one sentence: "For [user], who needs [job], we provide [product] that [key benefit]." If you cannot, the scope is not ready.

    In short

    A tight scope is not about doing less. It is about learning sooner, spending less and giving your first users something that works.
